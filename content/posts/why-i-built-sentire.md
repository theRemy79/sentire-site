I’ve had hearing loss all my life. Over the past 10–11 years, my hearing has fluctuated.

Those are parts of my hearing story. What I wanted was a practical way to keep the record of that story together: my audiograms, the dates of my tests, and a way to look back at how the measurements had changed.

That need is why I created Sentire.

## Having a report and having a history are different things

A hearing test gives you a view of your hearing at a particular time. When you have years of results, the question becomes bigger: how do you bring those individual views together?

For me, being able to access a report wasn’t the same as having an easy way to track my hearing history. I wanted to organize my audiograms, put tests in date order, and compare the measurements without having to reconstruct the history each time.

Medical portals can be useful places to retrieve records. But the view I wanted was a personal one, built around my hearing over time. I wanted to be able to bring my records together in a place I could keep using, alongside the care I receive.

The original reports still matter. Sentire is a way to organize and work with the measurements from them, while keeping those source reports available to check.

## Why fluctuating hearing made that matter to me

When my hearing has fluctuated over a decade, a single report is only one part of the record. I wanted a way to look at more than one test and follow the history across dates.

Sentire grew from that practical need: making my hearing-test records easier to keep and revisit.

> I wanted a clearer record of my hearing, with my own audiograms at the center of it.

## What I wanted from a hearing history tracker

The idea started with a few straightforward needs:

- **Keep tests together.** Give each audiogram a date and a place in a personal hearing history.
- **Compare measurements over time.** Look back at earlier tests alongside later ones, with the test details still available.
- **Keep the details that matter.** Record the ear, frequency, hearing level, and other information supplied by the report, rather than reducing everything to one summary number.
- **Bring a useful record to an appointment.** Export a report that I can choose to share and discuss with my audiologist.
- **Keep control of the files.** Store hearing records on my computer and make my own backups.

Those needs became the basis for Sentire’s timeline, comparisons, notes, reports, and backup tools.

## What Sentire does today

Sentire is a personal desktop application for organizing hearing-test records. You can enter measurements yourself or start with an existing PDF, JPG, or PNG audiogram.

The import tools run locally and can suggest measurements from supported report layouts. Those suggestions are a starting point for review. You need to check them against the original report and correct or add anything that was missed before saving.

Once tests are saved, you can follow them in a hearing timeline, compare records, and keep notes with the individual tests. You can also export a PDF report or save a backup of your history.

The [field guide](/guide/) shows how that works, including the review steps and the limits of importing a report. I want those limits to be clear: an organized record is useful only when the information in it has been checked.

## A personal record to bring into the conversation

Sentire organizes measurements from hearing tests you’ve already had. It doesn’t perform a hearing test, diagnose a condition, or decide what a change means for your care.

That conversation belongs with your audiologist or other hearing-care professional. My aim with Sentire is to make it easier to bring your existing history into that conversation, with the records and original reports available to refer to.

Your hearing records stay on your computer. Purchase and initial license activation use the internet; after activation, the application can be used offline. Keep your original reports and independent backups as well. The [privacy page](/privacy/) explains the distinction between local hearing records and the separate purchase and activation service.
